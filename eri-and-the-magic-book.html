Eri and the Magic Book

A Tansley Tales StoryCreated and read by Tania JacobsWords and pictures made with AI, then edited and checked by Tania

Adventure story in chaptersReading together12 min read

Eri loves books. Her little sister Marie can’t read yet. When they find a warm red book with no name in the library, it draws them a map to a secret door, and an adventure the two sisters can only finish together.

Chapter 1Stickers and Stories

Eri was eight years old, and she loved books.

She read at breakfast.

She read in the car.

She even read in the bath. But only once.

Her little sister, Marie, was five.

Marie loved two things.

Stickers and snacks.

Marie could not read yet.

But she could stick.

She stuck a star on the fridge.

She stuck a frog on Dad’s shoe.

She stuck a bunny on Eri’s book.

“Marie! Not on my book!” said Eri.

“Sorry,” said Marie.

She did not look very sorry.

“Library day!” called Mum.

“Yay!” said Eri.

“Can I get a book about bunnies?” asked Marie.

“You can’t read,” said Eri.

Marie looked down at her shoes.

“She can’t read yet,” said Dad. “Yet is a little word, but it does a big job.”

Marie put her stickers in one pocket.

She put two biscuits in the other.

“Just in case,” she said.

The library was big and quiet.

It smelled of paper and polish.

“Quiet voices,” said Mum.

“Walking feet,” said Dad.

“And stay together, you two,” said Mum.

Mum and Dad sat down in the big soft chairs.

The children’s corner was right beside them.

Eri ran her finger along the bottom shelf.

Bump, bump, bump.

Then she stopped.

There was a small red book.

It had no name on the front.

It had no name on the back.

And it was warm, like toast.

Eri opened it.

The pages were empty.

“That is a boring book,” said Marie.

Then a thin black line wiggled across the page.

It drew a shelf.

It drew a chair.

It drew a tiny bunny.

“It’s a map!” whispered Eri. “A map of our library!”

Marie pointed at the tiny bunny.

“That’s the bunny shelf,” she said. “I know where that is.”

Under the map, words began to grow.

Eri read them out, one by one.

“Children only. Knock three times.”

Chapter 2The Little Red Door

Marie led the way.

She used her walking feet. It was not easy.

The bunny shelf stood at the far end of the children’s corner.

Eri pulled out one bunny book.

Then another.

CLICK!

The shelf swung open like a gate.

Behind it was a little red door.

It was just as tall as Marie, and no taller.

It had a round gold handle.

“A grown-up would have to crawl,” said Eri.

“A grown-up would get stuck,” said Marie.

Eri looked back.

Mum was reading.

Dad was reading.

The big library clock said ten o’clock.

Eri’s tummy felt fizzy.

“Stay together,” she said.

She held out her hand, and Marie took it.

Knock. Knock. Knock.

The little red door swung open.

Inside was a round room, glowing with golden lanterns.

Books went up and up, all the way to the ceiling.

In the middle stood a chair shaped like a duck.

On the chair sat a big grey cat.

He wore tiny round glasses on the end of his nose.

“Ah,” said the cat. “Readers. At last.”

Eri and Marie jumped.

“You can talk!” said Eri.

“Of course I can talk,” said the cat. “I live in a library. I have had plenty of time to learn the words.”

He bowed his furry head.

“I am Sir Whiskers, Keeper of the Children’s Room.”

“Why is it only for children?” asked Eri.

“Grown-ups have the whole library,” said Sir Whiskers. “This room is yours.”

He winked.

“Besides, grown-ups never find the door. They look up high. Children look down low. All the best things are down low.”

“Can we stay?” asked Marie.

“You may,” said Sir Whiskers. “But first, you must answer three questions.”

Chapter 3Three Questions

Sir Whiskers cleaned his glasses on his tail.

“Question one,” he said. “I have a spine, but no bones. I can take you far away, but I never move. What am I?”

Eri’s hand shot up.

“A book!” she said.

“Correct,” said Sir Whiskers.

“Question two. I have no words at all, but I can tell you a whole story. What am I?”

Eri thought.

She thought some more.

No words? Every story she knew was full of words.

“I don’t know,” she said at last.

Eri did not like not knowing.

Marie tugged her sleeve.

“A picture,” said Marie. “A picture tells a story.”

“Correct!” said Sir Whiskers.

Eri stared at her little sister.

“How did you know that?” she asked.

“I read pictures all the time,” said Marie.

“Question three,” said Sir Whiskers. “This one is very important. What is round, and sweet, and lovely with a cup of tea?”

“A biscuit!” shouted both girls.

“That is not a riddle,” said Eri.

“No,” said Sir Whiskers. “I am just hungry.”

Marie reached into her pocket.

She held out a biscuit. It was a bit squashed.

Sir Whiskers ate it in two bites and purred like a little engine.

“Now,” he said, licking his whiskers. “You may open the Magic Book.”

Chapter 4The Giggle Page

“Where is it?” asked Eri.

She looked up at the tall shelves.

“You are holding it,” said Sir Whiskers.

Eri looked down.

The little red book was glowing.

“The Magic Book chooses its own readers,” said Sir Whiskers. “Today, it chose you two.”

Eri opened the book.

Now there were words inside, curly and gold.

She read them out loud.

“Giggle, wiggle, snort and squeak,

Tickle every nose and cheek!”

POOF!

Glitter puffed into the air.

BOING!

A big yellow rubber duck bounced past.

A drawer slid open.

Out marched a stripy sock.

Then another.

The socks began to dance.

Marie laughed until she got the hiccups.

Eri laughed so hard she had to sit down on the floor.

“Ah,” said Sir Whiskers happily. “The giggle page. My favourite.”

BOING!

Another duck bounced by.

BOING! BOING!

Then two more.

Now there were ten socks, dancing in a long, wiggly line.

The glitter was up to their ankles.

“That is enough now,” said Eri.

But the magic did not stop.

Eri shut the book.

It sprang open.

She shut it again and sat on it.

FLUMP!

It sprang open and tipped her onto the floor.

“Sir Whiskers!” called Eri. “How do we make it stop?”

But Sir Whiskers was laughing too hard to help.

He rolled right off the duck chair.

“Last page!” he squeaked. “Hee hee! Look at the last page!”

Eri turned to the last page.

Her tummy went cold.

“There are no words,” she said. “It’s only a picture. I can’t read it!”

A duck bounced off her head.

BOING!

“I can,” said Marie.

Chapter 5The Gold Star

Marie looked at the picture.

She looked hard, the way she always looked at pictures.

There was a red book.

The book was shut.

And on its cover sat one gold star.

“It wants a star,” said Marie. “A star to keep it shut.”

She reached into her pocket.

She peeled off her very best gold star.

Eri shut the book and held it tight with both hands.

Marie pressed the star onto the cover.

The book gave a small, happy sigh.

The ducks sat down.

The socks folded themselves into pairs.

The glitter floated down like snow.

Everything was still.

“You did it,” said Eri. “You read the last page.”

“But I can’t read,” said Marie.

“Not yet,” said Eri. “Not words. But you read pictures better than anyone I know.”

Marie stood up very tall.

Sir Whiskers climbed back onto the duck chair.

He put his glasses straight.

“One of you reads words,” he said. “One of you reads pictures. A good book needs both. That is why it chose the two of you.”

Sir Whiskers pulled a book from the lowest shelf.

There were bunnies on every page.

“For our picture reader,” he said. “Borrow it. Bring it back next week.”

Marie hugged it to her chest.

The girls squeezed onto the duck chair together.

Eri read the words.

Marie read the pictures.

Sir Whiskers ate the second biscuit and listened with his eyes shut.

Then the lanterns began to dim.

“Time to close the room,” said Sir Whiskers.

“Can we come back?” asked Eri.

“Whenever you like,” said Sir Whiskers. “Knock three times. And always come together.”

“Can we bring Mum and Dad?” asked Marie.

Sir Whiskers shook his head.

“Tell them all about it,” he said. “But they will not find the door. This room is just for children. It always has been.”

“And the Magic Book?” asked Eri.

“Put it back on the bottom shelf,” said Sir Whiskers. “It will find you when it has a new page ready.”

Chapter 6Glitter

The little red door closed with a click.

The bunny shelf swung back into place.

The big library clock still said ten o’clock.

Not one minute had gone by.

Eri slid the little red book back onto the bottom shelf.

Its gold star twinkled.

Mum looked up from her book.

“Did you find something good?” she asked.

“A secret room!” said Marie. “And a talking cat! And ducks! And dancing socks!”

“It’s behind the bunny shelf,” said Eri.

Dad got down on his hands and knees.

He peered behind the bunny shelf.

He saw a wall.

“No door here,” he said, smiling. “But what a wonderful story, you two.”

“Marie,” said Mum, “why is there glitter in your hair?”

Eri and Marie looked at each other.

They giggled.

That night, Marie climbed onto Eri’s bed with the bunny book.

She stuck a bunny sticker on Eri’s pillow.

This time, Eri did not mind at all.

“You read the words,” said Marie. “I’ll read the pictures.”

And that is just what they did.

“Next week,” whispered Eri, “we bring more biscuits.”

“And more stickers,” said Marie.

Far away, on the bottom shelf of the library, a little red book gave one tiny giggle.

The End
