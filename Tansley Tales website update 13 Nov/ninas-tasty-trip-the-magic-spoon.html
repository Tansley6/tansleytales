Nina’s Tasty Trip: The Magic Spoon

Story timeFood and culture12 min read

Nina loves food, and she wants to know what children eat in other parts of the world. A magic spoon takes her on a tasty trip around the globe, where she learns to say thank you in many languages.

Once upon a time, in a snug little town between rolling hills, there lived a bright-eyed girl named Nina.

Nina adored food. Sweet, salty, crunchy, squishy, and everything in between!

She loved helping Grandma in the kitchen. She stirred the pots. She kneaded the dough. And she tasted everything.

One sunny afternoon, as they baked biscuits together, Nina asked, “Grandma, what do children eat in other parts of the world?”

Grandma’s eyes twinkled. She opened a drawer and took out a shiny silver spoon.

“This is no ordinary spoon, my dear,” she said.

“It’s a magic spoon. Hold it tight and say the magic words.”

Glow, little spoon, glow, glow, glow!

Where in the world will we go?

The spoon began to shine.

WHOOSH!

Nina was swept up into the sky, spinning like a leaf in the wind.

Nina landed in a sunny village by the sea. She was in Italy!

The air smelled of warm tomatoes, garlic and fresh basil.

A cheerful boy named Luca waved from a bakery door. He held out a slice of pizza, straight from the oven.

Nina took a big bite. “It’s warm and cheesy and stretchy!” she giggled.

“Pizza was first made here in Italy, in a city called Naples,” said Luca proudly.

Then Luca showed Nina how to spin pizza dough high in the air.

Up it went. Round and round.

FLOP! It landed on Nina’s head.

They laughed until their tummies hurt.

“Grazie, Luca!” said Nina. That means thank you in Italian.

Nina held her spoon tight.

Glow, little spoon, glow, glow, glow!

Where in the world will we go?

WHOOSH!

Nina landed in a quiet garden full of pink cherry blossoms. She was in Japan!

Petals floated down like snow, and a little stream went trickle, trickle.

A girl named Aiko smiled and gave a little bow. She held out a small plate of sushi.

“Sushi is made with sticky rice, fish and seaweed,” said Aiko. “We dip it in soy sauce.”

Nina took a bite. “It’s cool and soft and salty, like the sea!”

Then Aiko handed Nina two chopsticks.

Nina tried to pick up a piece of sushi.

Wobble, wobble. PLOP! Back on the plate.

She tried again. Wobble, wobble... got it! Aiko clapped.

“Arigato, Aiko!” said Nina. That means thank you in Japanese.

Nina held her spoon tight.

Glow, little spoon, glow, glow, glow!

Where in the world will we go?

WHOOSH!

Nina landed in a busy market full of olives, spices and warm bread. She was in Palestine!

Stallholders called out, and the smell of herbs drifted through the air.

A girl named Layla waved from her family’s bakery. She gave Nina a round, warm flatbread called mana’eesh.

“It’s sprinkled with za’atar, a mix of thyme, sesame seeds and sumac,” said Layla. “We eat it for breakfast.”

Nina took a big bite. “It’s soft and crispy and herby!”

Then Layla showed Nina how to mix za’atar with golden olive oil.

Stir, stir, stir. They spread it on fresh dough, ready for the oven.

They sipped cool mint lemonade while it baked.

“Shukran, Layla!” said Nina. That means thank you in Arabic.

Nina held her spoon tight.

Glow, little spoon, glow, glow, glow!

Where in the world will we go?

WHOOSH!

Nina landed in a sunny square full of music and laughter. She was in Spain!

A guitar played, and children chased pigeons around a fountain.

A boy named Mateo waved her over to a long table. His whole family was sitting down for lunch.

“Try our tortilla española,” said Mateo. “It’s a thick omelette made with potatoes and eggs.”

Nina took a bite. “It’s soft and golden and so filling!”

Then Mateo’s grandpa showed them a trick.

He put a plate on top of the pan and... FLIP! The tortilla turned right over.

“Olé!” cheered everyone.

“In Spain, the whole family eats lunch together,” said Mateo.

“Gracias, Mateo!” said Nina. That means thank you in Spanish.

Nina held her spoon tight.

Glow, little spoon, glow, glow, glow!

Where in the world will we go?

WHOOSH!

Nina landed in a bright town full of colour. She was in Mexico!

Paper flags in pink, blue and yellow fluttered above the street.

A boy named Diego handed her a taco. It was a soft corn tortilla, filled with beans, cheese and salsa.

Nina took a bite. “It’s soft and cheesy, and a little bit spicy!”

“Tacos can have lots of different fillings,” said Diego. “Everyone has a favourite.”

Then Diego showed Nina how to make guacamole.

They squished and mashed ripe green avocados.

Squish, squash, SPLAT! A blob landed on Diego’s nose.

They laughed and laughed.

“Gracias, Diego!” said Nina. “We speak Spanish here too,” Diego grinned.

Nina held her spoon tight.

Glow, little spoon, glow, glow, glow!

Where in the world will we go?

WHOOSH!

Nina landed on a busy street full of bicycle bells and bright colours. She was in India!

The air smelled of cumin, ginger and warm spices.

A girl named Priya took Nina’s hand and led her home. Her mum had cooked a big pot of dal, a warm, golden lentil stew.

“We scoop it up with chapati,” said Priya. “That’s our flatbread.”

Nina tore off some chapati and scooped up the dal. “It’s warm and cosy, like a hug!”

Then Priya showed Nina how to roll chapatis into circles.

Priya’s was perfectly round.

Nina’s looked like a map of the world!

“That’s the best one,” laughed Priya.

“Dhanyavaad, Priya!” said Nina. That means thank you in Hindi.

Nina held her spoon tight.

Glow, little spoon, glow, glow, glow!

Where in the world will we go?

WHOOSH!

Nina landed in a courtyard full of music and dancing. She was in Nigeria!

Drums went boom-ba-boom, and everyone wore bright, patterned clothes. It was a party!

A boy named Tunde handed her a bowl of jollof rice.

“Jollof is cooked with tomatoes, peppers and spices,” said Tunde. “We always have it at parties!”

Nina took a big bite. “It’s smoky and tasty, and a little bit spicy!”

Then Tunde’s auntie let them stir the big party pot.

Round and round went the long wooden spoon.

Nina’s magic spoon gave a tiny, jealous wobble.

Then everyone danced while the rice bubbled.

“E se, Tunde!” said Nina. That means thank you in Yoruba.

Nina held her spoon tight.

Glow, little spoon, glow, glow, glow!

Where in the world will we go?

WHOOSH!

Nina landed in a snowy forest, beside a little wooden cabin. She was in Canada!

Snow crunched under her feet, and smoke curled from the chimney.

A girl named Emilie waved from the door. “Bonjour! Come in, it’s pancake time!”

Inside, there were stacks of pancakes and golden maple syrup.

“Maple syrup comes from maple trees,” said Emilie. “We boil the sap until it’s thick and sweet.”

Nina took a bite. “It’s fluffy and sticky and sweet!”

Then Emilie’s dad poured hot syrup onto clean snow outside.

The girls waited, then rolled it up on sticks like lollies.

“It’s a sweet made by winter!” laughed Nina.

“Merci, Emilie!” said Nina. That means thank you in French.

Nina’s tummy was full. Her heart was full too.

She held her spoon tight and whispered,

Glow, little spoon, glow, glow, glow!

Now it’s time for home, you know.

WHOOSH!

Nina landed back in Grandma’s kitchen, just as the biscuits came out of the oven.

“Well?” asked Grandma. “What did you learn, my sweet girl?”

Nina threw her arms around her.

“Grazie. Arigato. Shukran. Gracias. Dhanyavaad. E se. Merci,” she said.

Grandma laughed. “What was all that?”

“It’s thank you, Grandma,” said Nina. “In every place I visited. Food is different everywhere, but it’s always made with love.”

Grandma smiled. “That’s the magic of food, Nina. It brings people together, no matter where they’re from.”

The magic spoon gave a tiny twinkle.

“Grandma,” said Nina, “can we cook something from every place I visited?”

And that is exactly what they did.

The End
