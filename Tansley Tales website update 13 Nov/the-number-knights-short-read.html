The Number Knights and the Missing Crystal

In the land of Numeria, numbers were everywhere.

They were on the doors. They were on the clocks.

They were on the buns in the baker’s shop.

High on a tower sat the Great Maths Crystal.

It made every number shine.

But one morning, the crystal was gone!

The numbers went wobbly.

The clock said banana o’clock.

“Oh dear,” said King Zero. “Call the Number Knights!”

Clip, clop, clip, clop. Here they came.

Sir Plus, who loved to add.

Lady Minus, who loved to take away.

Sir Times, who loved lots and lots.

Lady Share, who loved to share.

And at the back, on a very small pony, came Equals.

Equals was not a knight yet.

She carried the snacks. She carried the golden scales.

And she always checked that both sides were the same.

“We will find the crystal!” said the knights.

“Me too,” said Equals, very quietly.

Soon they came to a river.

The bridge had a broken plank and a big gap.

“Easy!” said Sir Plus. “We need 3 new planks. 1 and 2 more makes 3.”

Bang, bang, bang. The bridge was mended.

Equals held up her scales. “1 and 2 is the same as 3,” she said.

But nobody heard.

Can you count them?

Next they met a dragon and his baby brother.

They had 8 slices of pizza, and they were cross.

“He has more!” “No, HE has more!”

“Easy!” said Lady Share. “8 slices, 2 dragons. 4 for you, and 4 for you.”

Equals held up her scales. “4 is the same as 4,” she said.

But nobody heard.

The happy dragon pointed across the sea.

“Something shiny went that way!”

By the sea were 4 pirates and a little boat.

“We will row you across,” said Captain Dot.

“But the boat is too full.”

There were 7 cannonballs in the boat.

It could only carry 5.

“Easy!” said Lady Minus. “7 take away 2 leaves 5.”

Thud, thud. Out came 2 cannonballs.

Can you count them?

“And rowing is hungry work,” said Captain Dot.

“2 biscuits for each pirate.”

“Easy!” said Sir Times. “4 pirates. 4 lots of 2 is 8.”

Equals opened the snack bag and counted out 8 biscuits.

“4 lots of 2 is the same as 8,” she said.

“Thank you, small person,” said Captain Dot.

He was the first to hear her all day.

Can you count them?

At last they came to the Castle of Shapes.

The big door was shut tight.

In front of it stood a giant pair of scales.

One side held 6 golden stones.

The other side held 4.

Over the door were five words: MAKE BOTH SIDES THE SAME.

“Add lots!” said Sir Plus.

“Take lots away!” said Lady Minus.

“Lots of lots!” said Sir Times.

“Share them out!” said Lady Share.

What a muddle! The scales went up and down, and up and down.

“STOP!” said Equals.

Everybody stopped.

Can you count them?

“This side has 6,” said Equals. “That side has 4. And 4 and 2 more is the same as 6.”

She put 2 golden stones on the scales.

Clunk.

Both sides were the same.

Creeeak. The door opened.

Inside sat the Shape Wizard, with the crystal in his hands.

“I only borrowed it,” he said sadly. “Nobody ever comes to do puzzles with me.”

“Then come home with us,” said Equals.

“We have lots of puzzles. And snacks.”

So the crystal went home, and the Wizard came too.

The numbers shone again.

The clock said six o’clock.

King Zero tapped Equals gently on the shoulder with his sword.

“Arise, Lady Equals, Knight of Numeria,” he said.

“Without you, a sum is only a question.”

Now there are five Number Knights.

Plus, Minus, Times and Share.

And Equals, who makes it fair.

So if a sum ever makes your tummy feel wobbly, remember what the Number Knights say.

Maths is not a monster. It is a puzzle.

And puzzles are best when we solve them together.

The End
