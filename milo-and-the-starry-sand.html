Milo and the Starry Sand

A Tansley Tales StoryCreated and read by Tania JacobsWords and pictures made with AI, then edited and checked by Tania

Bedtime storyCourage7 min read

Milo is a tiny, quiet mouse who loves to draw pictures in the sand, but he is far too shy to show anyone. When a storm ruins the Big Talent Show, Milo has to decide whether to hide or to help.

In the middle of Willow Wood, where the willow trees whisper and the stream runs slow, there lived a tiny mouse called Milo.

Milo was small.

Milo was quiet.

And Milo had a secret.

Every morning, when the wood was still yawning, Milo crept down to the sandy bank by the stream.

He picked up his favourite twig and began to draw.

Scritch, scratch, swish and sway,

Milo drew his worries away.

He drew moons and mountains. He drew fish with fancy fins. He drew a bear wearing a very small hat.

But whenever a leaf rustled or a twig snapped, Milo dashed behind a stone and hid.

One day, a poster went up on the Big Oak Tree.

THE BIG TALENT SHOW! Saturday. Moonrise. Everyone welcome!

The whole wood was buzzing.

Squirrel practised juggling acorns. (Bonk! Sorry, Badger!)

Frog practised singing. (Mostly burping.)

The Rabbit twins practised a dance that went hop, hop, flop.

Everyone was excited. Everyone except Milo.

“I’m too small to be seen,” he whispered. “And too quiet to be heard.”

The next morning, Milo was drawing a starry sky when a voice said,

“OH. MY. GOODNESS!”

Milo nearly jumped out of his whiskers.

It was Luna the Ladybird, spotty and bright, and she did not stop talking.

“Did you draw this? You did! You drew this! It’s the most wonderful thing I’ve ever seen, and I’ve seen a LOT of things, because I fly, you know, and flying means seeing, and seeing means...”

“Hoo,” said a soft voice from above. “Breathe, Luna.”

It was Ollie the Owl.

Ollie looked down at the drawings for a long, long time.

“Milo,” he said gently, “you have a gift.”

“You must show everyone at the Talent Show!” cried Luna. “You simply MUST!”

Milo’s ears drooped. His tail curled up tight.

“But what if they laugh?” he whispered.

“What if they don’t look?

What if I’m too small, too quiet, too... me?”

Just then, someone arrived. Very slowly.

It was Toby the Tortoise.

“Sorry I’m late,” said Toby. “I set off on Tuesday.”

(It was Thursday.)

Toby looked at Milo’s worried face and smiled a slow, kind smile.

“Bravery doesn’t come all at once, little one,” he said.

“Slow and steady.

One small step.

Then you’re ready.”

Milo thought about that all night long.

Scritch, scratch, swish and sway,

Milo drew his worries away.

On Saturday, the wood looked magical.

The animals hung painted leaf banners between the branches. They strung up berry lanterns. They built a stage on the sandy clearing by the stream.

Milo watched from behind a mushroom.

“Maybe next year,” he told himself. “When I’m bigger. When I’m louder. When I’m braver.”

Then the sky grew grey.

The wind began to howl.

And down came the rain.

Pitter, patter.

Splish, SPLASH.

Crackle, rumble, CRASH!

The animals ran for cover. The banners flapped and tore. The lanterns rolled away. The paint washed off in wiggly, wobbly streams.

When the storm was over, everyone crept out.

The stage decorations were gone. Every single one.

“The show is ruined,” sniffed Rabbit.

“No banners,” sighed Badger.

“No lanterns,” groaned Squirrel.

“Ribbit,” said Frog, which meant, “I don’t mind, honestly.”

Milo looked at the clearing.

The rain had left the sand smooth, and flat, and shining.

Just like a brand new page.

Milo’s heart went thump, thump, thump.

He could hide. Hiding was easy.

Or he could help. Helping was hard.

He remembered Toby’s words.

Slow and steady. One small step.

Milo took one small step out from behind the mushroom.

Then another.

Then another.

“I... I have an idea,” he squeaked.

Nobody heard.

“EVERYONE!” shouted Luna. “MILO HAS AN IDEA!”

Every head turned. Every eye looked at Milo.

His knees wobbled. His whiskers wobbled. Even his tail wobbled.

But he held up his twig and said, a little louder this time,

“I can draw the decorations. In the sand.”

Nobody laughed.

Ollie simply nodded. “Show us, Milo.”

So Milo drew.

Scritch, scratch, swish and sway,

Milo drew the gloom away.

He drew a giant silver moon. He drew stars that swirled like snowflakes. He drew willow trees with long, curling branches all around the stage.

The animals helped. Squirrel pressed shiny acorns in for sparkle. Rabbit tucked in flower petals. Toby laid pebbles very carefully in a line. (It took quite a while.)

And when the real moon rose, the wet sand glittered like a sky full of stars.

“Ooooooh,” said everyone.

“Aaaaaah,” said everyone.

“Ribbit,” said Frog, which meant, “Wow.”

The show went on. Squirrel juggled (only one bonk). The Rabbit twins danced (only one flop). Frog sang (only three burps).

And at the very end, Ollie called out,

“And our final act... the artist who saved the show... MILO!”

Milo stood in the middle of his moonlit drawing.

He was still small.

He was still quiet.

But everyone was cheering. For him.

Milo didn’t need to be big, or loud. He just needed to believe in what he could do.

That night, snuggled in his bed beneath the willow roots, Milo smiled a sleepy smile.

Scritch, scratch, swish and sway,

Milo dreamed his brave new day.

Because Milo knew something now, and so do you:

Even the smallest voices can make a big difference.

The End
